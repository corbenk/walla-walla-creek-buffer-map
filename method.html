How we computed the buffer numbers

Computed 2026-10-10 from the City of Walla Walla's and Walla Walla County's own public GIS services. Nothing here is an official determination; see the disclaimer on the map.

What the map shows

  • Stream centerlines and the 100-ft buffer band are read live, every time the page loads, from the City of Walla Walla GIS Group's public map service WW_Streams_Riparian_Buffer (published 2026-10-06). The 100-ft band is the City's own polygon, named by the City "Visible Streams Buffer 100 Feet From Stream Centerline."
  • The 35, 50, 75 and 150-ft comparison bands are computed in your browser by offsetting the same City centerlines (Turf.js). They are for comparison only.
  • Parcels are read live, one at a time, from Walla Walla County's public Tax Parcels service. The share of a parcel inside the band is computed in your browser from the parcel polygon and the band polygon.
  • No City or County geometry is copied to this website.

Reconciling with the City consultant's figures

The City's consultant (Facet, July 2026) reports the buffer area growing from 268.8 to 757.4 acres on 43.2 stream miles (29.7 open + 13.5 piped). The map's band is 477 acres on 17.9 miles.

These are both the City's numbers, and they measure different things. Facet's stream network is roughly 2.4 times longer than the City's Oct 6 "visible streams" layer: it includes piped reaches (13.5 miles), and, by its length, additional open reaches that the visible-streams layer does not draw (the USGS National Hydrography Dataset shows about 36 miles of flowlines inside city limits, and County hydrography about 28). 43.2 miles buffered 100 ft on each side is about 1,047 acres before accounting for overlaps and the city boundary, which is consistent with Facet's 757 acres inside the city. The map band should therefore be read as the minimum extent on mapped open channels; the consultant's figure is the broader estimate the City is working from.

A figure of "1,100 acres" that has circulated is not in any City document we found; 42 miles corresponds to Facet's 43.2.

Caveats

  • Centerline vs. ordinary high-water mark. Every band on this map, including the City's own, is measured from a digitized stream centerline. The draft ordinance measures from the ordinary high-water mark, top of bank, or channel migration zone, whichever is greater. For a channel 20 to 60 ft wide, the regulated edge is 10 to 30 ft farther out than drawn. That is why the result card flags lots within 150 ft as "possibly affected."
  • Positional accuracy. The City's centerlines are attributed "Placed on Map" (digitized from imagery, not surveyed). Expect tens of feet of uncertainty; a result within about 30 ft of a threshold should be treated as undetermined.
  • Which streams. The City layer draws visible open channels inside city limits only: it omits Mill Creek, piped reaches, and all streams in the unincorporated Urban Growth Area, although the draft ordinance also covers "intermittent open channels between piped sections" and the UGA. The City's layer contains every creek named in draft Table 6 except Reser Creek, which lies outside city limits; it also includes Butcher, Lincoln, College, Barber and Kathy creeks, three springs and one short irrigation segment under the draft's 'and others within city limits' language, and it excludes Mill Creek (Shoreline Master Program). Audit (2026-10-10, data/scripts/audit_streams.py): 61 segments, 17.94 miles, all inside city limits; one segment flagged intermittent. Outside city limits the map returns "outside city limits" rather than a distance: we deliberately do not add County or State hydrography for the growth area, because it is different data under different (County) rules.
  • Parcel data is the County Assessor's working layer (2026-09-28). Owner names are never requested or displayed.
  • Only the City of Walla Walla can determine whether a specific property is subject to critical-areas rules. Confirm with Development Services (509-524-4710, permits@wallawallawa.gov).
