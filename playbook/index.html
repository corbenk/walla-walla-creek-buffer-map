CreekChecker County Playbook

How to give any Washington county what Walla Walla got: a lot-by-lot creek buffer map, a plain-language brief, and a calm, sourced way for neighbors to speak up before their council or commission decides.

The opportunity

Every county updates on the same state clock

State law requires every county and city to review its critical areas rules on a staggered schedule, and creek buffers are changing in many of the drafts we have read. No state law sets a creek-buffer width, and a city under 25,000 people may adopt its county’s rules by reference, so in towns that do, the county’s choice is the town’s choice.

  • Dec 31, 202610 counties: Benton, Chelan, Cowlitz, Douglas, Franklin, Kittitas, Skamania, Spokane, Walla Walla, Yakima
  • June 30, 202715 counties, including nearby Columbia, Garfield, Asotin and Whitman
  • Dec 31, 202510 counties, deadline passed

Inputs

What a county launch needs

  1. A local lead who knows the people and will post in their own voice.
  2. The draft: the proposed critical areas amendments (the stream or riparian table), the staff report, and the hearing calendar.
  3. Map layers: streams, any proposed buffer layer, tax parcels, city limits and urban growth areas. Most counties publish these as public map services.
  4. Contacts: the clerk or commission address that puts comments in the record, and the group address for the elected body.
  5. Three local stories: a lot, a street, a rebuild question.
  6. Partners: the Realtor board, home builders, farm bureau, conservation district, chamber and local newsrooms.

Sequence

Six weeks from first look to hearing

  1. Week 0Find the draft and the next hearing date. Open a facts file.
  2. Week 1Wire the map layers; count the lots and homes touched; draft the brief.
  3. Week 2Fact-check every sentence; build the email draft and print pieces.
  4. Week 3Launch: the local lead posts, partners forward, one press pitch.
  5. Weeks 4–5Comment drive (“a line or two about your lot”) and speaker sign-up.
  6. Hearing weekCalendar file, Zoom and phone details from the posted agenda, short speaker scripts.
  7. Day afterUpdate the site from the meeting video within 24 hours; thank people.

Credibility

Rules every county keeps

  1. Every public number traces to a source in the county’s facts file. Unverified means unused.
  2. The map is an awareness tool, not a legal determination, and says so on every view.
  3. Grace in headlines. Assume good faith of staff and elected officials; say what we are for.
  4. State the other side fairly: fish-bearing creeks and the law’s special consideration for salmon and steelhead.
  5. No fear-mongering. Say what the draft does to building, additions and rebuilds.
  6. Paid ads only after Meta’s social-issue authorization, with a “Paid for by” line. Nothing is posted or sent on anyone’s behalf without their OK.

Measures

How we know it worked

Lot checks, emails opened, written comments in the record and hearing speakers, and decisions that change: local science commissioned, buffers tiered by stream, written rebuild protections, a delayed effective date, or options requested before a vote.
