Your next step · City · inside the mapped band

Before you build, repair or rebuild.

An inside-band result raises different questions for an existing home, an addition and a new building. Start with the answers below, then ask the City to confirm how the rules would apply to your plans.

Questions that match this result

Can my existing house stay?per draft ordinance

Yes. Legally built homes and structures continue as nonconforming structures under city code chapter 20.212, which the draft leaves in place. Nothing requires you to move, remove, or plant anything on day one. The new rules apply when you apply for a permit to build, expand, or rebuild. If adopted Oct 28, the ordinance takes effect Dec 31, 2026.

Can I repair or maintain what I have?per draft ordinance

Yes. The draft allows maintenance and repair of existing buildings, fences, roads and utilities as long as the work does not encroach further into the buffer, and lets you keep mowing, weeding, pruning, gardening, and maintaining pathways and patios as they existed before adoption. Fences can be replaced or newly built.

Can I add on, or build a shop, shed, ADU, or new patio inside the band?not yet confirmed

The draft says new structures must be outside the buffer and the 15-foot setback, and it has no rule for additions to homes already inside the band. Existing city code (20.212) lets a nonconforming building extend along its existing line but not closer to the stream. Whether a landward addition or ADU on a house inside the band will be allowed, or will need a critical-areas report and a buffer reduction (averaging and enhancement reductions both stop at 75 feet on a 100-foot buffer; a separate rule lets the director exclude buffer areas cut off by pavement or buildings) or a reasonable-use exception, has not been answered in writing. A new patio or uncovered deck is allowed in the 15-foot setback but not in the buffer.

If my house burns down, can I rebuild?per existing city code

Yes, if you apply within six months. The draft itself is silent and defers to existing city code 20.212, which says a damaged nonconforming structure loses its grandfathered status unless a building-permit application to repair or rebuild is filed within six months of the damage. Under that same existing code, reconstruction costing more than 25% of replacement value also needs a conditional-use permit. Miss the six-month window and the structure loses its grandfathered status, so a rebuild would have to meet the new 100-foot buffer and setback. The draft adds no fix, although residents and a commissioner asked for one at the Sept 14 hearing.

Your next step · City · near the mapped edge

Find the real line before making plans.

A result near the edge needs a closer look. Read how the mapped line differs from the regulated starting point, and ask what is needed to locate that line on your property.

Questions that match this result

Why might I still be affected?per draft ordinance

The regulated buffer starts at the ordinary high water mark (the edge of the channel), top of bank, or channel migration zone, not the centerline shown here, so it extends farther than this map draws. Only a field survey by a qualified professional sets the real line, and the City has published no parcel list.

What is the 15-foot setback?per draft ordinance

The draft requires buildings to sit 15 feet back from the buffer edge, up from 5 feet today; the director can reduce it to 5 feet with a critical-areas report. Landscaping, uncovered decks, roof overhangs up to 24 inches, walkways, patios, and rain gardens are allowed in that setback; buildings are not.

Your next step · City · outside the mapped band

Know what this result does—and does not—answer.

The map result is a starting point, not a permit clearance. If you have work in mind, check other applicable rules with the City. You can also take part in the broader proposal discussion.

Questions that match this result

Why should I care?per draft ordinance

The City's own consultant (Facet, July 2026, in the Council packet) says the buffer area nearly triples, from 269 to 757 acres (2.1% to 5.8% of the city), on 43.2 stream miles. Our count on the City's own GIS layers shows about 1,490 parcels inside city limits touching the band on mapped creeks, up from about 980 at today's 35 feet. The City must plan for 2,348 new homes by 2046, state middle-housing, ADU and lot-split laws exclude lots inside critical-area buffers, and no one has counted what the band subtracts.

Did the City's own Planning Commission support this?per draft ordinance

No. On Sept 14, 2026, after 13 people testified, the Planning Commission voted 7-0 to recommend that Council direct staff to revise the draft to fit the City's infill goals using 'available science matching local conditions.' The draft posted Oct 9 is unchanged: 100 feet on every creek.

Why is my creek drawn differently here?per draft ordinance

Will the County do the same thing?typical in WA CAOs

The County has not published a draft yet. Its August 2026 buffer map is tiered rather than one width. Watch for its hearing dates this fall.

Your next step · Mill Creek · shoreline rules

Ask about the shoreline rules for your reach.

Your map result includes a Mill Creek note. Start with the separate shoreline explanation, then ask the City which provisions apply at your reach and to the work you have in mind.

Questions that match this result

What about Mill Creek?per draft ordinance

Mill Creek is regulated under the City's Shoreline Master Program, not the proposed Critical Areas Ordinance. The SMP sets a 35-foot buffer along the flood channel (Gose St to the Bennington Lake diversion) and shoreline jurisdiction extends 200 feet; the 100-foot creek buffer in the draft does not apply here.

Questions that match this result

Why is nothing drawn on my lot?not yet confirmed

College Place publishes no public GIS buffer layer. The County-mapped creek lines are shown for orientation only; the County's working buffer does not apply inside College Place.

Is College Place updating its code too?typical in WA CAOs

Every Growth Management Act city in the county is on the same Dec 31, 2026 periodic-update deadline, but we have not found a College Place draft. Ask College Place Planning.
